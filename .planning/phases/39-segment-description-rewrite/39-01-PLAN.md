**Voice and structure rules (from CONTEXT.md):**
- Observational naturalist voice — precise but evocative, not clipped trail-guide or literary
- Third-person only — NO "you", "you'll", "your"
- Fixed three-clause order: surface texture → ecology → terrain/riding character
- 35-55 words per description (count carefully)
- Surface woven as texture modifier in the first clause, NOT as a label ("Packed county gravel rolls south..." not "Surface: packed gravel")
- Common species names only — no Latin, no generic groups ("hardwoods", "conifers", "mixed forest")
- No road names, mile markers, landmarks, or restock points in prose (that info lives in segment headers/metadata)
- Landscape only — describe the trail and forest, not the rider

**Ecological reference (from RESEARCH.md) — use these species for each segment:**

**sector-520 (smooth asphalt):**
Surface: smooth asphalt. Ecology: northern hardwoods adjacent — sugar maple, yellow birch on upland slopes near Pictured Rocks corridor. Terrain: flat, fast, road riding.

**sector-nf2266 (sand and gravel two-track):**
Surface: deteriorating sand and gravel two-track. Ecology: late-seral northern hardwoods — sugar maple, beech dominant; red maple, cherry, yellow birch, basswood; white pine and hemlock interspersed. Multi-layered canopy. Terrain: deep sand, washboard ruts, relentless climbs.
NOTE: Use "mature northern hardwoods" NOT "old-growth" (unverifiable claim per RESEARCH.md Open Question 1).

**sector-bass-lake (packed gravel, county-maintained):**
Surface: packed gravel, county-maintained. Ecology: paper birch common on productive upland sands; red maple, aspen; alder and cedar along lake margins. Terrain: rolling, open, forgiving. Differentiator: the lake itself + paper birch character.

**sector-nf2217 (compact gravel and hard-packed dirt):**
Surface: compact gravel and hard-packed dirt doubletrack. Ecology: red pine and white pine corridor on flat outwash plain; cedar and tamarack along stream margins. Terrain: long, flat, meditative. Differentiator: Indian River headwaters + pine corridor (distinguish from Bass Lake's birch/lake character).

**sector-nd2225 (loose gravel transitioning to sandy patches):**
Surface: loose gravel and sandy patches, unmaintained. Ecology: jack pine dominant on dry sandy soils (outwash plains); oak, aspen, paper birch; blueberry understory. Terrain: quiet, remote, demanding attention. Differentiator: jack pine / blueberry — this is the classic barrens character.

**sector-doe-lake (deep sand and rugged two-track):**
Surface: deep sand, rugged two-track with roots and embedded rock. Ecology: red pine and jack pine on upland sands; cedar and tamarack near lake margins; aspen regeneration. Terrain: technical, punishing, short sharp climbs. Differentiator: roots, rocks, mixed conifer on moraines.

**sector-rapid-river (firm packed gravel, USFS-maintained):**
Surface: firm packed gravel truck trail. Ecology: northern hardwoods returning — sugar maple, yellow birch, beech on upper slopes; aspen on ridge flanks; white pine on ridges. Terrain: long descents, moraine topography. Differentiator: forest transitions back to hardwoods, homeward character.
