# Ecological Zone Data: Hiawatha's Revenge Route Segments

**Project:** Hiawatha's Revenge — Segment Description Rewrite (Ecological Focus)
**Researched:** 2026-04-06
**Domain:** Hiawatha National Forest, Alger County, Michigan Upper Peninsula
**Overall confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (LANDFIRE BpS data verified; segment-specific attribution is geographic inference)

---

## Research Context

This file documents the ecological zones along the Hiawatha's Revenge cycling route for use in rewriting 7 segment descriptions. The route runs ~100 miles through the Munising Ranger District of Hiawatha National Forest (West Unit), starting and ending in Munising, MI.

**Primary source:** Randy Swaty's 2023 LANDFIRE-powered assessment of Hiawatha National Forest
(`rswaty.github.io/hiawatha2023`) using LANDFIRE Biophysical Settings (BpS) and Existing
Vegetation Type (EVT) data. The BpS data represents historical potential vegetation; the EVT
data represents current mapped vegetation as of 2020.

### Geographic Pattern Across the Route

The route travels south from Munising (Lake Superior shoreline influence, moraines, hemlock-hardwood)
into the forest interior (flat glacial outwash plains, jack pine, peatlands) and back north.

The geological gradient matters:
- **North (near Munising, near Lake Superior):** Rolling moraines, glacial till, loamy soils. Mesic conditions favor northern hardwoods (sugar maple, yellow birch) with hemlock. Cedar swamps in depressions.
- **Mid-route (forest interior):** Flat sandy glacial outwash plains. Infertile, acidic, droughty soils. Jack pine, red pine, blueberry barrens. Peatlands and black spruce bogs in wetlands.
- **South-returning:** Transitional — sandy till moraines, mixed jack pine/hardwood, increasing wetland complexity as the route descends toward the Rapid River corridor.

Source: USFS description of Hiawatha "rolling hills forested with northern hardwoods, white pine and hemlock, flatland covered by red pine, jack pine and aspen, and large open and tree covered wetlands." Multiple sources agree (MEDIUM-HIGH confidence).

## BpS Type Reference — Species and Character

### Laurentian-Acadian Northern Hardwoods Forest-Hemlock (21% of forest)

**Where on route:** Dominant community around Munising and northern segments (520, NF2266 start, Ridge Rd end). Found on rolling moraines and glacial till.

**Canopy species:**
- Sugar maple (*Acer saccharum*) — dominant
- Eastern hemlock (*Tsuga canadensis*) — co-dominant to associate
- Yellow birch (*Betula alleghaniensis*) — common
- American beech (*Fagus grandifolia*) — largely absent in western Upper Peninsula due to cold winters
- Basswood (*Tilia americana*) — occasional
- Red maple (*Acer rubrum*) — common
- White pine (*Pinus strobus*) — frequent associate

**Understory:**
- Striped maple, mountain maple, beaked hazelnut, various dogwoods (shrub layer)
- Wild leek (*Allium tricoccum*), maidenhair fern, trilliums, violets (ground layer)
- Lady fern, oak fern — abundant
- Bunchberry, goldthread on moister sites

**Character:** Multi-generational closed canopy. Gap-phase disturbance dynamics — individual trees fall, opening small light gaps. Old trees create deep shade; floor covered in fern and wildflower carpets. Hemlock clusters on north-facing slopes and near streams create dark, mossy microhabitats. Hemlock was heavily harvested near Munising in the 1890s for tannin extraction; much current hemlock is second-growth.

**Soil:** Loamy sand to sandy loam on glacial moraines. Moderately acid.

Source: Michigan Natural Features Inventory mesic northern forest description, MSU Extension northern hardwood description (HIGH confidence).

---

### Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp (24% of forest)

**Where on route:** All segments cross swamp corridors. Most prominent flanking NF2266 (miles 5-18), Camp 7 Lake area (NF2217-2218, miles 32-50), Doe Lake approaches (miles 70-92).

**Canopy species (northern Michigan variant):**
- Northern white-cedar (*Thuja occidentalis*) — dominant on wet sites
- Hemlock (*Tsuga canadensis*) — co-dominant in richer swamps
- Black ash (*Fraxinus nigra*) — common
- Yellow birch (*Betula alleghaniensis*) — common
- Tamarack (*Larix laricina*) — present, especially in wetter areas
- White pine (*Pinus strobus*), red maple (*Acer rubrum*) — associates
- Balsam fir (*Abies balsamea*) — wet-site associate
- American elm (*Ulmus americana*) — occasional

**Understory:**
- Sphagnum and other mosses — extensive carpet
- Cinnamon fern (*Osmundastrum cinnamomeum*) — abundant
- Royal fern — wet microsites
- Skunk cabbage, marsh marigold — spring ephemeral
- Labrador tea, leatherleaf — in wetter pockets
- Northern white-cedar and tamarack roots form elevated hummock mats above inundated muck

**Character:** Dark, damp corridors. Standing water in spring and after rain. Strong cedar/fern smell. The hummock-and-hollow microtopography is distinctive — cedars on elevated root mats, open wet areas between. Riders will notice the road surface becomes soft where the road crosses swamp margins.

Source: Michigan Natural Features Inventory hardwood-conifer swamp, NatureServe Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp description (HIGH confidence).

---

### Boreal Acidic Peatland Systems (17% of forest)

**Where on route:** Flanking roads through the interior — most visible near Doe Lake segment (miles 70-92) and ND2225 (miles 50-70). Route skirts peat bogs rather than crossing them directly.

**Dominant species:**
- Black spruce (*Picea mariana*) — dominant tree; stunted (20-30 ft), often flag-form
- Tamarack (*Larix laricina*) — frequent co-dominant
- Open bog areas: no canopy, just shrubs and sphagnum

**Understory/ground layer:**
- Sphagnum moss — deep, spongy carpet (defines the community)
- Leatherleaf (*Chamaedaphne calyculata*) — dominant shrub
- Labrador tea (*Rhododendron groenlandicum*) — common
- Bog rosemary (*Andromeda polifolia*) — scattered
- Pitcher plant (*Sarracenia purpurea*), sundew (*Drosera* spp.) — carnivorous plants
- Bog laurel, blueberry, cranberry — heath shrubs

**Water chemistry:** Very acidic (pH < 4.2). Nutrient inputs from precipitation only (ombrotrophic bogs). Dark tannin-stained water where visible.

**Character:** Forested bogs have an otherworldly quality — sparse, stunted black spruce rising above a quaking sphagnum mat. Open bogs are flat, treeless, and wet. Both types typically visible from road as dark fringe flanking upland forest.

Source: Michigan Natural Features Inventory Bog description, Black Spruce silvics (USDA FS), Pennsylvania Natural Heritage Program black spruce-tamarack peatland description (HIGH confidence for species; route proximity is inference).

---

### Boreal Jack Pine-Black Spruce Forest-Pine Barrens (6% of forest)

**Where on route:** Sandy outwash flats through the mid-route interior. Most likely prominent along ND2225 (miles 50-70) and portions of NF2217-2218 where the terrain flattens onto glacial outwash.

**Canopy species:**
- Jack pine (*Pinus banksiana*) — dominant; short-lived (80-100 yr), 50-80 ft
- Red pine (*Pinus resinosa*) — common associate on slightly better soils
- Northern pin oak (*Quercus ellipsoidalis*) — dry barrens variant

**Understory:**
- Lowbush blueberry (*Vaccinium angustifolium*) — abundant
- Sweetfern (*Comptonia peregrina*) — characteristic shrub
- Bracken fern (*Pteridium aquilinum*) — often dense
- Reindeer lichen (*Cladonia* spp.) — extensive mats on bare sand
- Sand cherry (*Prunus pumila*), prairie willow — occasional shrubs
- Bear oak, hazelnut — scattered

**Soil:** Sandy, very acid, infertile, excessively drained. Pure sand in places — the "deep sand" that makes these roads ride so differently. Characteristic gray-white glacial outwash sand.

**Character:** Open, sun-drenched canopy compared to hardwood forest. Shorter trees, more sky. Ground appears almost garden-like: blueberry low scrub, patches of lichen-white sand. Fire historically maintained this community; suppression has allowed hardwood encroachment. The deep-sand roads through jack pine are the most distinctive riding surface on the route.

Source: Michigan Natural Features Inventory pine barrens description, MSU Extension jack pine forest type (HIGH confidence).

---

### Boreal White Spruce-Fir-Hardwood Forest (11% combined coastal + inland)

**Where on route:** Pockets through the mid-route, particularly where moisture-retaining soils support spruce-fir. May appear as sporadic stands along NF2266 (miles 5-18) and near Camp 7 Lake.

**Canopy species:**
- Balsam fir (*Abies balsamea*) — dominant
- White spruce (*Picea glauca*) — co-dominant
- Northern white-cedar (*Thuja occidentalis*) — wet-site associate
- Paper birch (*Betula papyrifera*) — important associate; increases post-disturbance
- Quaking aspen (*Populus tremuloides*) — post-disturbance
- White pine — occasional

**Understory:**
- Canadian yew (*Taxus canadensis*) — dense shade-tolerant shrub
- Bunchberry (*Cornus canadensis*) — common ground cover
- Goldthread (*Coptis trifolia*) — characteristic
- Twinflower (*Linnaea borealis*) — trailing vine
- Mosses, clubmosses, lichens — extensive
- Lady's-slipper orchids — occasional

**Character:** Coolest, darkest forest on the route. Dense balsam fir and spruce create almost tunnel-like passages. Distinctive smell — balsam resin and wet moss. Paper birch stands bright white against the dark conifers. Often found in frost pockets and north-facing slopes.

Source: MNFI boreal forest description, MSU Extension mesic conifers description, NatureServe eastern boreal balsam fir-white spruce-paper birch description (HIGH confidence).

---

### Laurentian-Acadian Pine-Hemlock-Hardwood Forest (5% of forest)

**Where on route:** Transition zones between upland hardwood and sandy pine communities. May appear on NF2217-2218 and portions of Bass Lake Rd where soil moisture varies.

**Character:** Mixed forest with hemlock, white pine, sugar maple, and yellow birch. A structurally complex community — tall emergent white pines above a hemlock-hardwood understory. Multiple canopy layers. Old-growth characteristics where undisturbed.

**Key species:** White pine (*Pinus strobus*), eastern hemlock, sugar maple, yellow birch, red maple, basswood. Ground layer: wood sorrel, Canada mayflower, bunchberry, ferns.

Source: NatureServe Laurentian-Acadian Pine-Hemlock-Hardwood Forest description (MEDIUM confidence — limited detail available).

## Segment-by-Segment Ecological Profiles

### Segment 1: 520 — Paved Road East of Munising
**Miles:** 0-1.3 (distance from start: ~1.1 mi)
**Surface:** Paved (M-28 / Route 520)
**Character:** Not gravel; included in route for navigation.

**Ecological zone:** Urban-forest edge. Munising sits on a bay at the foot of sandstone bluffs (Pictured Rocks geology). Exiting town eastward, the road passes through forest edge with sugar maple, yellow birch, and scattered white pine. The escarpment geology creates alkaline conditions favoring hemlock-hardwood above, with cedar swamps in low areas.

**BpS context:** Likely **Laurentian-Acadian Northern Hardwoods Forest-Hemlock** on upland slopes with **Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp** in low areas. Boreal coastal influence possible near Lake Superior shoreline.

**What riders see:**
- Sugar maple and yellow birch canopy with hemlock pockets near streams
- Brief glimpse of Lake Superior bay at start
- Forest edge character: some open areas, roadside grasses, then forest closes in

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — inference from Munising geology and general upland forest pattern near Lake Superior.

---

### Segment 2: NF2266 — Deep Sand Forest Service Road
**Miles:** ~5-18 (distance from start: 6.7 mi, length: 3.2 mi gravel segment within longer stretch)
**Surface:** Deep sand — 5-star difficulty
**Character:** The most technically demanding segment; deep, loose sand through forest interior.

**Ecological zone:** This is the primary transition zone from upland hemlock-hardwood into deeper forest interior. The deep sand surface indicates **glacial outwash or lakeplain soils** — the same substrate that supports jack pine and sandy forest types.

**BpS context:** Likely transitioning from **Laurentian-Acadian Northern Hardwoods Forest-Hemlock** to **Boreal Jack Pine-Black Spruce Forest** as the road moves away from moraine soils onto outwash. **Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp** flanks the road in depressions. The deep sand is diagnostic of the jack pine / dry northern forest zone.

**What riders see:**
- Sandy road surface — the sand is the ecological indicator here; it tells you exactly what soil type you are on
- Jack pine stands opening up as sandy soils dominate: shorter trees, more sky, blueberry and bracken fern ground cover
- Cedar swamp margins at low crossings: dark, wet, ferns
- Possible paper birch and aspen stands in disturbed or post-fire areas
- Reindeer lichen mats on the sandiest sections

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — deep sand is a direct indicator of outwash substrate; specific species mix inferred from substrate + LANDFIRE BpS data.

---

### Segment 3: Bass Lake Rd — Gravel Past Bass Lake
**Miles:** ~18-32 (distance from start: 25.3 mi, length: 4.8 mi gravel segment)
**Surface:** Maintained gravel road — 2-star difficulty
**Character:** More traveled gravel road through mixed forest with lake views.

**Ecological zone:** This segment passes Bass Lake, so water features and associated wetland vegetation are present. The road is better-maintained (2-star vs 5-star), suggesting more compact, non-outwash soils — likely glacial till moraine terrain with higher clay content, supporting richer forest types.

**BpS context:** Likely **Laurentian-Acadian Northern Hardwoods Forest-Hemlock** on moraine uplands with **Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp** around Bass Lake and stream crossings. The lake itself is **Open Water** BpS.

**What riders see:**
- Bass Lake visible through tree breaks — dark blue water framed by mixed forest
- Upland hardwood on moraine knolls: sugar maple, yellow birch, red maple canopy
- Hemlock in north-facing ravines: deep green, dark understory
- Cedar swamp margins at lake edge and stream crossings: cedar/tamarack mix, ferns
- Ground layer in uplands: ferns (ostrich fern possible in rich spots), trilliums in spring, wood sorrel, Canada mayflower

**Transition note:** This segment may represent the richest forest on the route — moraine soils with adequate moisture and nutrients support the most diverse northern hardwood composition.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — Bass Lake is a specific geographic feature; moraine soils inferred from 2-star road difficulty and position in route.

---

### Segment 4: NF2217-2218 — Forest Interior, Camp 7 Lake Area
**Miles:** ~32-50 (distance from start: 36.8 mi, length: 6.6 mi gravel segment)
**Surface:** Maintained gravel — 2-star difficulty
**Character:** Long interior forest segment passing through the Camp 7 Lake area (mile 44.7, water pump and vault toilet).

**Ecological zone:** Camp 7 Lake is a 60-acre interior forest lake. The route through this area penetrates deep into the forest interior. The flat terrain and lake proximity suggest a mix of upland forest and surrounding wetland complexes.

**BpS context:** Multiple BpS types likely present. The Camp 7 Lake area likely has **Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp** around the lake margins, **Boreal Acidic Peatland Systems** in lower areas, and **Laurentian-Acadian Northern Hardwoods Forest-Hemlock** on upland ridges between wetlands.

**What riders see:**
- Camp 7 Lake — a clear interior lake with forested shores (cedar, balsam fir, white spruce at lake edge)
- Mixed forest interior: alternating upland hardwood ridges (sugar maple, hemlock, yellow birch) and wetland corridors
- Cedar swamp crossings: the road crosses swamp drainages where northern white-cedar and balsam fir create dark, wet passages
- Possible spruce-fir stands in low spots: white spruce and balsam fir with bunchberry ground cover
- Black spruce fringe at peat bog margins — watch for the character shift from tall hardwoods to stunted dark spruce at drainage edges

**Ecological transition:** This is the deepest forest zone on the route — the interior plateau where the hardwood-wetland mosaic is most intact.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — Camp 7 Lake is a specific feature; BpS attribution is geographic inference from position in route + forest-wide distribution data.

### Segment 5: ND2225 — Loose Gravel Heading South
**Miles:** ~50-70 (distance from start: 55.7 mi, length: 3.9 mi gravel segment)
**Surface:** Loose gravel — 3-star difficulty
**Character:** The route begins heading south through forest interior toward the Midway General Store (mile 75.7).

**Ecological zone:** Moving south through forest interior on loose gravel suggests the route is on sandy terrain with mixed soil conditions. The "loose gravel" surface type (vs. deep sand) indicates slightly coarser or more rocky substrate — potentially a transition zone between sandy outwash and glacial till.

**BpS context:** Likely **Boreal Jack Pine-Black Spruce Forest-Pine Barrens** on sandy uplands with **Boreal Acidic Peatland Systems** in flanking wetlands. This is the zone most likely to show the classic jack pine-peatland mosaic that characterizes the forest interior.

**What riders see:**
- Jack pine stands: shorter canopy, open sky, blueberry and bracken fern understory
- Sweetfern on dry roadside shoulders — an almost infallible indicator of sandy, acidic, infertile soil
- Reindeer lichen patches where sand is most exposed
- Peat bog margins at drainages: abrupt transition from upland pine to stunted black spruce and sphagnum
- Possible red pine stands where soils are slightly better — taller, redder-barked trees contrasting with shorter jack pine

**Ecological character:** The most boreal-feeling segment on the route. Open sky through jack pine, lichen-white sand, the dark void of a peat bog fringe — this is distinctly north-country ecology.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — sandy substrate inferred from loose gravel surface; species mix strongly supported by LANDFIRE data and forest interior position.

### Segment 6: Doe Lake — Sandy Two-Track Near Doe Lake
**Miles:** ~70-92 (distance from start: 84.8 mi, length: 3.1 mi gravel segment)
**Surface:** Sandy two-track — 4-star difficulty
**Character:** Sandy, narrow two-track near Doe Lake, approaching from the Midway General Store (mile 75.7). The return to deep sand (4-star) signals return to outwash substrate.

**Ecological zone:** The Doe Lake area and approach from the south involves some of the most remote terrain on the route. The return to sandy two-track indicates sandy outwash or lakeplain soils similar to NF2266.

**BpS context:** Likely **Boreal Jack Pine-Black Spruce Forest-Pine Barrens** dominant on uplands around Doe Lake, with **Boreal Acidic Peatland Systems** at the lake edges and surrounding lowlands. **Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp** possible in richer, wetter depressions.

**Doe Lake character:** Interior lakes in this zone typically have black spruce-sphagnum peatland margins transitioning to open water. Pitcher plants and sundews at lake edge. Leatherleaf and Labrador tea shrubs. The lake water may be dark (tannin-stained) from peat drainage.

**What riders see:**
- Two-track through jack pine and mixed sandy forest
- Doe Lake: a dark-water interior lake with peatland margins
- Carnivorous plants (pitcher plant, sundew) at bog margins if road approaches closely
- Sphagnum moss and leatherleaf shrub flanking wet sections
- Blueberry, sweetfern, bracken fern on dry sandy upland sections
- Possible red pine plantation sections — straight rows, open understory, needle duff

**Ecological transition:** Coming north from Doe Lake, the route begins its return toward moraine terrain. Watch for the subtle shift: deeper sand gives way to slightly rockier or more loamy substrate, and jack pine starts mixing with more maple and birch.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — Doe Lake is a specific geographic feature; BpS attribution inference from surface type + interior lake ecology.

### Segment 7: Ridge Rd (Rapid River Truck Trail) — Return to Munising
**Miles:** ~92-110 (distance from start: 94.6 mi, length: 6.3 mi gravel segment)
**Surface:** Truck trail, gravel — 2-star difficulty
**Character:** The return route back toward Munising on a truck trail (wide, maintained, low-difficulty). This segment was renamed from "Rapid River Truck Trail" to "Ridge Rd" in recent work.

**Ecological zone:** The return toward Munising and Lake Superior shifts back to moraine terrain and richer forest types. The "ridge" name suggests topographic relief — moraine ridges with better-drained upland soils.

**BpS context:** **Laurentian-Acadian Northern Hardwoods Forest-Hemlock** dominant on moraine ridges. **Laurentian-Acadian Alkaline Conifer-Hardwood Swamp** in swales and depressions between ridges. Possible **Boreal White Spruce-Fir-Hardwood Forest-Inland** in frost pockets or north-facing slopes.

**What riders see:**
- Return to taller, denser hardwood forest: sugar maple canopy reasserting itself
- Hemlock in north-facing ravines and along stream drainages
- Cedar swamp in low crossings between ridges
- Late-season: maple canopy turning yellow and orange above the dark green hemlock understory — the quintessential Upper Michigan fall color combination
- Fern understory: ostrich fern in rich spots, cinnamon fern at wetland margins
- Yellow birch boles: distinctive peeling amber-gold bark on moraine uplands
- Possible white pine emergents: tall individuals rising above the hardwood canopy where old-growth influence remains

**Ecological transition:** This segment completes the full ecological loop — from the moraine hemlock-hardwood of Munising, through the sandy interior jack pine and peatland mosaic, back to moraine hardwood-hemlock on the return. The contrast between the route's two halves (deep forest interior vs. moraine upland) is the defining ecological narrative of Hiawatha's Revenge.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — moraine terrain inferred from "Ridge" in segment name + position returning toward Munising; species confirmed by BpS data.

## Ecological Transitions Summary

| Between Segments | Transition | Observable Sign |
|-----------------|------------|-----------------|
| 520 → NF2266 | Moraine upland to sandy outwash | Road surface changes from paved to deep sand; canopy shortens; sugar maple gives way to jack pine |
| NF2266 → Bass Lake Rd | Sandy outwash to mixed moraine | Road firms up; taller canopy returns; sugar maple and yellow birch reappear |
| Bass Lake Rd → NF2217-2218 | Moraine forest to interior wetland mosaic | Lake views; cedar swamp crossings become more frequent; peat bog fringe appears |
| NF2217-2218 → ND2225 | Interior mosaic to sandy jack pine | Road becomes looser; canopy opens; blueberry and sweetfern dominant ground layer |
| ND2225 → Doe Lake | Sandy jack pine to peatland-lake margin | Two-track narrows; dark water visible through trees; sphagnum and leatherleaf at road edge |
| Doe Lake → Ridge Rd | Peatland/outwash to returning moraine | Sand firms; canopy height increases; maple and birch increase; hemlock reappears |

## Gaps and Confidence Notes

**What is HIGH confidence:**
- LANDFIRE BpS distribution percentages for the entire Hiawatha NF (from official data)
- Species composition within each BpS type (from MNFI, MSU Extension, USDA FS silvics — multiple verified sources)
- The general north-south ecological gradient (moraine hardwood → interior sandy pine → back)

**What is MEDIUM confidence (geographic inference):**
- Which specific BpS types dominate each segment — inferred from surface type, topographic name, proximity to water features, and general geographic position in the route
- Exact location of BpS transitions along the route — without GIS overlay of BpS data against the GPX track, segment-specific attribution is spatial inference

**What requires field verification or GIS analysis to confirm:**
- Exact mile positions of ecological zone transitions
- Whether specific stands (e.g., hemlock grove at a specific crossing) are present
- Whether the Doe Lake segment closely approaches bog margins or remains on upland
- Plantation vs. natural forest distribution along each segment (red pine plantations exist in Hiawatha NF and would differ from natural jack pine)

**Approach used:** Route geography (surface type, topographic names, proximity to named water features, route elevation data), LANDFIRE distribution data, and Michigan forest ecology literature were combined to produce the most likely ecological profile for each segment. This is analogous to using soil maps and topography to predict forest type — reasonable inference, but not a substitute for direct observation.
